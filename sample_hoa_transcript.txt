GREENWOOD VILLAGE HOMEOWNERS ASSOCIATION
MONTHLY BOARD MEETING TRANSCRIPT

Time: 6:00 PM - 8:30 PM
Location: Community Center, Main Hall

Board Members:

- David Chen, Vice President
- Rebecca Torres, Treasurer
- James Anderson, Secretary
- Lisa Patel, Member at Large

Management:
- Michael Roberts, Property Manager (GreenSpace Management)

Residents in Attendance:

===== MEETING BEGINS =====

Sarah Mitchell (President): Good evening, everyone. I'm calling this meeting to order at 6:05 PM. Thank you all for attending tonight. We have quite a full agenda, so let's dive right in. James, can you please read the minutes from our December meeting?

James Anderson (Secretary): Certainly, Sarah. In December, we discussed the holiday lighting contest results, approved the snow removal contract with Arctic Snow Services for $45,000, and tabled the discussion about updating our architectural guidelines. The minutes were distributed via email last week. Do we have any corrections or additions?

David Chen (Vice President): I believe we should add that we also approved emergency repairs to the north gate entrance. The cost was $3,200 from our reserve fund.

James Anderson: You're absolutely right, David. I'll amend the minutes to reflect that. Any other corrections?

[No response]

Sarah Mitchell: Hearing none, all in favor of approving the minutes as amended?

[All board members vote yes]

Sarah Mitchell: Motion carries. Let's move to our first agenda item - the financial report. Rebecca, the floor is yours.

Rebecca Torres (Treasurer): Thank you, Sarah. I'm pleased to report that our financial position remains strong. As of December 31st, 2023, our operating account holds $287,450, and our reserve fund stands at $1.2 million. This represents a 3% increase in reserves compared to last year.

For the fourth quarter, we collected 97.8% of HOA dues on time, which is slightly above our target of 97%. We have 12 units currently delinquent on their assessments, totaling $28,600. Our collection attorney has sent final notices to these homeowners, and we anticipate resolution within the next 30 days.

Regarding major expenses for Q4: snow removal came in at $12,300, which is $2,700 under budget due to the mild November. Landscaping was right on target at $18,500. Our insurance premiums increased by 8% as we discussed, bringing the annual cost to $156,000. The good news is that's still below the market average for comparable communities.

One concern I want to highlight: our common area electricity costs are running 15% over budget. Michael, can you address this?

Michael Roberts (Property Manager): Yes, Rebecca. We've noticed this trend as well. After investigation, we found that the lighting in the north parking lot has been staying on 24/7 due to a malfunctioning photocell sensor. We've already ordered the replacement part, which should arrive by Friday. Additionally, we've conducted an energy audit and found that upgrading to LED lighting in all common areas could save us approximately $8,000 annually. The upfront investment would be around $22,000, giving us a payback period of 2.75 years.

David Chen: That sounds like a worthwhile investment. Can we get a formal proposal for the February meeting?

Michael Roberts: Absolutely. I'll have three competitive bids ready for your review by February 1st.

Rebecca Torres: Thank you, Michael. Moving on to our reserve study update. As you know, we commissioned Reserve Advisors to conduct our annual review. The study indicates we should increase our monthly reserve contributions by $12 per unit starting in July to maintain our funding goal of 80%. This translates to an additional $28,800 annually across our 240 units.

The study also recommends that we accelerate our roof replacement program. Currently, we have 18 buildings scheduled for replacement between 2025 and 2027. However, recent inspections show that Buildings 7, 12, and 15 are deteriorating faster than anticipated due to the severe hailstorm we had in August. The study recommends moving these three buildings to 2024, which would require an additional $185,000 from reserves.

Sarah Mitchell: That's a significant expense. Do we have any warranty claims we can pursue for the hail damage?

Rebecca Torres: Our insurance deductible is $50,000 per occurrence, and the damage assessment came in at $47,500, so we're just under the threshold. However, I've asked our insurance broker to review the policy for any other coverage options.

Lisa Patel (Member at Large): I'd like to suggest we consider impact-resistant roofing materials for these replacements. Yes, they cost about 20% more upfront, but they typically come with better warranties and could reduce our insurance premiums. Plus, some insurance companies offer discounts for impact-resistant roofing.

Rebecca Torres: That's an excellent point, Lisa. I'll ask the roofing contractors to include impact-resistant options in their bids. It could pay for itself through insurance savings alone.

Maria Gonzalez (Unit 178, Resident): Excuse me, I have a question about the reserve assessment increase. You mentioned $12 per unit per month. Our current monthly assessment is $385. Is this increase on top of the regular annual adjustment?

Rebecca Torres: Good question, Maria. Yes, this would be in addition to our standard cost-of-living adjustment, which typically runs 2-3% annually. So homeowners should expect their monthly assessment to increase from $385 to approximately $405 starting in July - that includes both the reserve contribution increase and the COLA adjustment.

Maria Gonzalez: I understand the need, but that's a 5.2% increase. Many residents are on fixed incomes. Is there any way to phase this in over two years?

Sarah Mitchell: That's a valid concern, Maria. However, our reserve study is clear that we need to maintain adequate funding to avoid special assessments down the road. A smaller monthly increase is much more manageable than a sudden $5,000 special assessment if we defer critical repairs. That said, we can certainly discuss payment plans for residents experiencing hardship. Michael, can you prepare information about our hardship program for the next newsletter?

Michael Roberts: Absolutely. We currently have provisions for payment plans up to 12 months for assessment increases. I'll make sure this information is prominently featured in next month's newsletter.

Sarah Mitchell: Thank you. Any other questions on the financial report before we move on?

[No response]

Sarah Mitchell: Hearing none, let's move to agenda item three - maintenance and repairs. Michael, what's on the radar?

Michael Roberts: Thank you, Sarah. I have several updates. First, the elevator modernization project in Buildings 1 through 6 is now 85% complete. We're on schedule to finish by February 28th. The total project cost is tracking at $324,000, which is $8,000 under our budgeted amount of $332,000. Residents have been very understanding about the temporary inconveniences, though we did receive several complaints from elderly residents in Building 4 about the extended outage last week.

To address this, we provided temporary ground-floor accommodations for three residents who have mobility issues, and we've adjusted the schedule to ensure no building is without elevator service for more than 72 hours at a time.

James Anderson: What about the pool resurfacing project we discussed last fall?

Michael Roberts: Good timing on that question. I've received three bids for the pool resurfacing and tile replacement. The bids range from $78,000 to $92,000. I recommend we go with AquaTech Solutions at $82,500. They've done excellent work for several other HOAs I manage, and their timeline allows us to complete the work before Memorial Day weekend, ensuring the pool is ready for the summer season.

The scope includes complete resurfacing with a pebble finish, new tile along the waterline, replacement of all drain covers to meet current safety codes, and repainting the pool deck. They also identified that our pool heater is nearing end-of-life and recommend budgeting $12,000 for replacement within the next 18 months.

David Chen: I move that we approve the AquaTech Solutions proposal for $82,500, contingent on verification of their insurance and licensing.

Lisa Patel: I second that motion.

Sarah Mitchell: All in favor?

[All board members vote yes]

Sarah Mitchell: Motion carries. Michael, please proceed with finalizing the contract. What else do we have?

Michael Roberts: We've completed the annual fire extinguisher inspection across all buildings. Out of 380 units, 23 failed inspection - either empty, expired, or missing entirely. We've sent notices to those homeowners requiring compliance within 30 days. This is actually an improvement from last year when we had 41 failures.

Additionally, our landscaping contractor, GreenScapes Plus, has submitted their proposal for 2024. They're requesting a 6% increase, bringing the annual contract from $96,000 to $101,760. This includes the standard services plus monthly flower bed rotation and seasonal color installation that we added last year, which residents really appreciated.

However, I did solicit two additional bids as a comparison. The other proposals came in at $98,500 and $107,200. GreenScapes has been reliable, but the $98,500 bid from Nature's Best Landscaping is worth considering - they have excellent references and include some additional services like quarterly mulch replenishment.

Robert Williams (Unit 245, Resident): I'd like to speak to this if I may. I've lived here for eight years, and GreenScapes has been consistently excellent. Last summer when we had that drought, they were proactive about adjusting irrigation schedules and suggesting drought-tolerant plants. I'd hate to lose that relationship to save $3,260, especially when we're managing a million-dollar reserve fund.

Thomas Brown (Unit 302, Resident): I have to respectfully disagree with Robert. My background is in facilities management, and in my experience, vendor complacency is real. A 6% increase is substantial - that's double the inflation rate. I think we should at least interview Nature's Best and see what they bring to the table. Competition keeps vendors honest and innovative.

Sarah Mitchell: Both excellent points. I propose we table this decision until February, interview representatives from both companies, and make an informed decision then. Any objections?

[No objections]

Sarah Mitchell: Great. Michael, can you arrange for both vendors to present at our February meeting?

Michael Roberts: Will do. One more item under maintenance - we've had recurring issues with the storm drains in the east parking lot. During heavy rains, we get significant pooling. I had a civil engineer assess the situation, and he recommends regrading the parking lot and adding two additional catch basins. The estimated cost is $34,000. This wasn't in our 2024 budget, but it's becoming a safety issue. We've had two minor fender benders in that area during rainstorms, and I'm concerned about liability.

Rebecca Torres: Can we use reserve funds for this, or should this come from our contingency fund?

Jennifer Kim (Assistant Property Manager): I consulted with our reserve study advisor, and storm drain improvements would qualify as a common area capital improvement, so reserve funds would be appropriate. However, our contingency fund currently has $45,000 allocated, so we could use that and preserve reserves if the board prefers.

David Chen: I think this is exactly what contingency funds are for - unexpected but necessary repairs. I move we approve up to $35,000 from the contingency fund for the storm drain improvements.

Rebecca Torres: I second that.

Sarah Mitchell: All in favor?

[All board members vote yes]

Sarah Mitchell: Motion approved. Michael, please move forward with that project. Let's transition to agenda item four - architectural review committee updates. Lisa, you've been leading that committee. What do you have for us?

Lisa Patel: Thank you, Sarah. We've had a busy quarter. The ARC received 28 modification requests, approved 24, denied 2, and 2 are still pending additional information. The approved requests include 8 solar panel installations, 6 patio enclosures, 4 fence replacements, 3 window replacements, and 3 exterior paint changes.

The two denials were both for satellite dish installations in front-facing locations, which violate our architectural guidelines. We directed those homeowners to the approved rear-placement options.

I want to highlight a trend we're seeing: there's growing interest in electric vehicle charging station installations. We've approved 5 so far, but we don't have formal guidelines for EV chargers in our architectural standards. This is becoming an issue because installations vary widely - some are simple 240V outlets, others are wall-mounted units, and we're even getting questions about shared charging stations in common areas.

I recommend we form a special committee to develop EV charging guidelines and explore the possibility of installing common area charging stations. This could actually be a revenue generator for the HOA if we charge a reasonable usage fee.

David Chen: I fully support this. I recently installed a Level 2 charger myself, and the process highlighted how unclear our current guidelines are. I'd be happy to serve on this committee.

Sarah Mitchell: Excellent. Let's form a three-person committee - Lisa as chair, David, and we'll need one more volunteer. Are any residents interested?

John Martinez (Unit 289, Resident): I'd like to volunteer. I work in the automotive industry and have some expertise in EV infrastructure. Plus, I'm planning to purchase an electric vehicle this spring, so I have a personal interest in seeing this done right.

Sarah Mitchell: Perfect. Thank you, John. Lisa, David, and John will form our EV Infrastructure Committee. Please develop preliminary guidelines and cost estimates for our March meeting.

Lisa Patel: Will do. One more thing - we need to update our architectural guidelines document. The current version is from 2018, and we've made several amendments since then. I propose we engage an attorney to review and consolidate all amendments into an updated master document. The cost would be approximately $3,500.

James Anderson: I think that's money well spent. Having a clear, consolidated document will reduce confusion and potential disputes. I move we approve up to $4,000 for professional legal review and updating of our architectural guidelines.

Lisa Patel: I second that.

Sarah Mitchell: All in favor?

[All board members vote yes]

Sarah Mitchell: Motion carries. Let's move to agenda item five - security and safety updates. This has been a hot topic in our community emails lately.

Michael Roberts: Yes, we've received numerous concerns about vehicle break-ins over the past two months. We've had seven reported incidents, primarily in the west parking lot between 11 PM and 4 AM. I've been working closely with the local police department. They've increased patrols in our area, and we've submitted a request for additional street lighting to the city.

In the meantime, I recommend we consider upgrading our security camera system. Our current system has cameras at the main entrances and pool area, but coverage is limited. I've obtained proposals for a comprehensive upgrade that would add 16 additional cameras covering all parking lots and building entrances. The cost is $48,000 for equipment and installation, plus $2,400 annually for cloud storage and monitoring.

Patricia Lee (Unit 156, Resident): I'm one of the residents who had their car broken into. They smashed my window and stole a GPS unit. The police said having video footage would significantly improve the chances of catching these criminals and preventing future incidents. I strongly support this camera upgrade.

Thomas Brown: I agree with the need, but $48,000 is substantial. Have we considered less expensive alternatives, like motion-sensor lighting or community patrol volunteers?

Michael Roberts: We've actually already upgraded to LED motion-sensor lighting in three parking lots last year, which has helped. As for community patrols, our insurance carrier strongly discourages volunteer patrol programs due to liability concerns. They did indicate that enhanced camera systems could potentially reduce our insurance premiums by 3-5%, which would be $4,680 to $7,800 annually. That would offset the annual monitoring costs and provide a partial return on investment.

Rebecca Torres: From a financial perspective, if we achieve even a 3% insurance reduction, we're looking at a 10-year payback period on the initial investment, not counting the value of the security improvement itself. I think it's justified.

Sarah Mitchell: I agree. However, this is a significant expense, and I think our residents should have input. I propose we send out a community survey gauging interest and support for this camera upgrade, along with the financial details. We can vote on this at our February meeting after gathering community feedback.

David Chen: That sounds reasonable. I move we table the camera system decision pending a community survey.

James Anderson: Second.

Sarah Mitchell: All in favor?

[All board members vote yes]

Sarah Mitchell: Michael, please prepare and distribute that survey within the next two weeks. Moving on to agenda item six - community events and amenities. Jennifer, you've been coordinating our events. What's the update?

Jennifer Kim: Thank you, Sarah. Our fall social calendar was very successful. The Halloween party had 87 attendees, the Thanksgiving potluck had 62, and the holiday lighting contest drew participation from 34 units. Feedback has been overwhelmingly positive.

For 2024, we're planning a spring garden party in April, a summer pool party in July, and we'd like to add a new back-to-school event in August for families with children. Total projected cost for the year's events is $6,800, which is within our budgeted amount of $7,500 for community activities.

We've also received several requests to upgrade the playground equipment. The current equipment is 12 years old and, while still safe, is showing wear. I've obtained preliminary quotes for a complete playground renovation ranging from $45,000 to $68,000. The higher-end option includes ADA-accessible equipment and a shade structure.

Maria Gonzalez: As a grandparent who frequently watches her grandchildren, I can tell you the playground definitely needs updating. The equipment is limited, and there's no shade. On hot days, the metal slides are too hot to use. I'd strongly support a renovation that includes shade structures.

Sarah Mitchell: This is exactly the type of long-term planning we need to be doing. Jennifer, can you form a playground committee to develop a comprehensive proposal including equipment options, safety surfacing, shade structures, and ADA accessibility? We should aim to have this ready for a vote at our April meeting, with potential installation over the summer.

Jennifer Kim: Absolutely. I'll recruit volunteers through the newsletter.

Sarah Mitchell: Perfect. Let's move to new business. We have two items on the agenda. First, updating our pet policy. We've received several requests to increase the pet weight limit from 40 pounds to 60 pounds. Currently, we have 156 registered pets in our community, and we've had very few pet-related issues.

James Anderson: I've researched what other comparable communities in our area are doing. Most have weight limits between 50 and 75 pounds. Some have shifted to a breed-based restriction list instead of weight limits, focusing on aggressive breeds regardless of size.

Robert Williams: I'd like to speak in favor of increasing the limit. I have a 55-pound Golden Retriever who I had to register as an emotional support animal to comply with the current policy. She's the gentlest dog you'd ever meet. Weight isn't always a good indicator of behavior or appropriateness.

Thomas Brown: I have to disagree. I live on the first floor, and I can hear every footstep of the large dog living above me. Bigger dogs mean more noise, more wear on common areas, and potentially more liability. I think 40 pounds is reasonable.

Sarah Mitchell: This is clearly a divisive issue. I propose we include this question in the security survey we're already sending out - let's get community input on whether to increase the pet weight limit to 60 pounds, keep it at 40 pounds, or switch to a breed-restriction model instead.

David Chen: That makes sense. Let's gather data before making a decision that affects the entire community.

Sarah Mitchell: Any objections?

[No objections]

Sarah Mitchell: Great. Final item under new business - we've received a formal request from a group of residents to convert the unused tennis court into a multi-purpose sport court that could accommodate pickleball, basketball, and tennis with portable nets. Michael, what's the feasibility?

Michael Roberts: I had a sports surfacing company assess our tennis court. It hasn't been used for tennis in over three years - our usage logs show zero reservations in the past 18 months. Converting it to a multi-purpose court would cost approximately $28,000, including new surfacing, line striping for multiple sports, and portable net systems. The company estimates this would extend the life of the court surface by 10-12 years.

Patricia Lee: I'm part of the group that requested this. We have at least 15 residents interested in playing pickleball, but our tennis court is in poor condition and lacks the right dimensions. A multi-purpose court would serve more residents and get much more use.

Lisa Patel: I support this concept, but I'm concerned about noise. Pickleball can be quite loud. We need to consider the proximity to residential units.

Michael Roberts: The nearest building is about 75 feet away, and the court would be surrounded by existing fencing. We could add acoustic fencing panels if noise becomes an issue - that would be an additional $6,000 to $8,000.

Sarah Mitchell: This seems like a smart use of an underutilized amenity. I propose we move forward with getting detailed proposals, including noise mitigation options, for review at our March meeting.

Rebecca Torres: Before we do that, I'd like to see usage data and commitment from the residents requesting this. Can we have the interested residents form a pickleball club and demonstrate sustained interest over the next few months?

Patricia Lee: That's fair. We'd be happy to organize and show that there's genuine, ongoing interest.

Sarah Mitchell: Excellent. Patricia, please work with Jennifer to establish the club and track participation. We'll revisit this at our April meeting with usage data in hand.

Any other new business?

[No response]

Sarah Mitchell: Hearing none, let's move to open forum. This is the time for residents to bring up concerns not on the agenda. Please keep comments to three minutes or less.

Maria Gonzalez: I'd like to raise a concern about the guest parking situation. The 48-hour limit on guest parking is becoming problematic for those of us who have elderly parents visiting for extended periods. Is there any flexibility in this policy?

Sarah Mitchell: That's a good question. Michael, what options do we have?

Michael Roberts: Our governing documents allow the board to issue temporary parking permits for hardship situations, including extended family visits, medical recovery periods, and home renovations. Residents can request permits through the management office. We just need to formalize the application process.

Maria Gonzalez: That would be very helpful. Thank you.

John Martinez: I want to bring up a safety issue with the speed bumps in the main drive. They're very aggressive, and I've seen several cars bottom out, including my own. Can we evaluate whether they're appropriately sized?

Michael Roberts: I'll have a traffic engineer assess them. If they're not to specification, we can have them adjusted.

Robert Williams: One more thing - any update on getting a community dog park? This has been discussed for years.

Sarah Mitchell: That's actually something we should explore as part of our longer-term planning. Jennifer, can you add this to the list for our strategic planning session in March?

Jennifer Kim: Will do.

Sarah Mitchell: Any other items?

[No response]

Sarah Mitchell: Hearing none, I want to thank everyone for their participation tonight. Our next board meeting is scheduled for February 19th at 6:00 PM, right here in the community center. The agenda will be posted one week prior.

Before we adjourn, I want to remind everyone that we're still looking for volunteers for our architectural review committee, events committee, and the new EV infrastructure committee. If you're interested, please contact Michael or Jennifer.

David Chen: I move that we adjourn.

Lisa Patel: Second.

Sarah Mitchell: All in favor?

[All board members vote yes]

Sarah Mitchell: This meeting is adjourned at 8:32 PM. Thank you all for coming.
